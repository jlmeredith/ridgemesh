## Display and decision contract

Actual relief is fixed at 1×: terrain X/Y, elevation and antenna AGL use the same metre scale. Exaggeration can make small slopes more visible, but adds no RF evidence and distorted the previous rendered antenna heights. It has been removed. Radio symbols remain readable markers; their mast geometry uses actual AGL. Ground paths, radio/site labels, antenna heights, contours, coordinates, network connections, roaming test positions and heat start enabled. Layers can disable detail; 2D and registered aerial/satellite comparison remain available. Fit, zoom, selection and movement work without leaving 3D.

The filled heatmap uses the weaker of the selected forward and return route margins at each 40 m receiver cell, with a fixed 0–60+ dB legend. At least 6 dB is classed strong, 0–6 dB marginal, no valid two-way route red and unknown inputs gray. No-route status wins even if the other direction has unknown margin. This is signal reserve above the assumed sensitivity after modeled losses and fade allowance, not RSSI, probability or measured delivery. Cells drape on the actual 10 m terrain triangles and are split at the terrain diagonal to avoid disappearing inside ridges or saddles. Display interpolation does not create extra RF samples.

The default field covers the whole modeled landscape, including approaches and wooded gaps beyond camps. Event-only display is an optional extent. Percentages, weakest served margin and selected-route relay fraction still use the 11.68 ha event-use target weights; coloring more land does not change the scoring denominator. Unknown or unserved targets are excluded from the **weakest served** margin but remain in area percentages. Relay-use percentage means the chosen route uses an intermediate radio; it is not the fraction of coverage gained by that relay. Dashed network connections show route relationships, not certified straight line of sight.

The workbench exposes the recommended fixed deployment, radio roles/sites/heights and scored tradeoff beside the map. R1 alone removes fixed support while preserving other RF/crew settings and roaming test points. Alternate-site previews move the existing physical R1 ID and show matching recomputed heat, placement/height changes and coverage deltas. Preview does not change saved equipment. Apply backs up the previous plan; restore recovers it. Move a selected radio by choosing a landscape point, or edit its height; the change recalculates coverage and locks that device for the next search. Budget and equipment constraints continue to drive the finite placement search. Roaming participant counts never create fixed coverage.

## MediumFast baseline and computed recommendation

AstralMesh's public field guide specifies US, MediumFast, three hops, primary AstralMesh and frequency slot 0/auto. [Meshtastic's official preset table](https://meshtastic.org/docs/overview/radio-settings/#presets) gives MediumFast SF9, 250 kHz, CR4/5 and a 148 dB link budget at +22 dBm with 0 dB antenna gain. The derived receiver threshold is **22 − 148 = −126 dBm**. This is a documented planning inference, not a manufacturer measurement or a guaranteed conservative receiver specification. It reduces otherwise identical link margins by exactly 6 dB relative to the old −132 dBm fallback. Receiver sensitivity and fade allowance remain separate editable settings. Per-radio overrides win.

Model `ridgemesh-rf-4.1` adds the explicit default threshold. Old custom RF scenarios retain −132 dBm fallback unless their owners change it; raw old-format bytes are preserved. Only the exact untouched previous generated recommendation upgrades automatically. Named, edited, deliberately empty and corrupt scenarios retain the prior preservation rules. Field-walk exports carry the actual sensitivity assumption rather than silently recording the old fallback.

[Frequency slot 0 uses channel-name hashing](https://meshtastic.org/docs/configuration/radio/lora/#frequency-slot); the RF model's 915 MHz is nominal propagation frequency, not a claimed exact AstralMesh carrier. Real firmware settings still need recording.

Generated starter `astral-mediumfast-start-v1` evaluates 495 layouts with one mandatory P1, two assumed camp-radio slots, budget three, two roaming participants and optional G3 excluded. It selects **P1 ROUTER at Stargazer, 1.5 m AGL**, and **fixed CLIENT_BASE at Harmony Grove, 3 m AGL**, leaving one radio in reserve. Under these assumptions, event samples are 100% strong with 100% having two modeled access options; the selected full network's weakest served margin is approximately 25.6 dB and about 22% of event area uses a selected relay route. These outputs are not field claims. R1 alone remains strong across the event targets under the baseline; comparing continuous margins and outer-landscape gaps makes its difference visible when the headline percentage saturates. Both access options still depend on the primary P1.

The bounded search objective is unchanged: weighted strong/marginal area, two access options, capped headroom, then fewer radios and lower total antenna height. Its lowest sufficient candidate height is not a mounting/solar recommendation. Stargazer's trees, available rigging, sunlight, stage permissions, canopy/body losses and actual radio settings remain field checks. Geography was not moved to improve the result; terrain `usgs-2021-10m-v1` and site `astral-source-reconciliation-20260913-v1` remain unchanged.

## Knowledge captured

Decision: open the computed solution in actual-scale 3D with a landscape margin field and visible scenario comparisons. Rationale: static pins and a saturated percentage do not explain deployment tradeoffs. Lessons: preserve comparison objectives and physical IDs; align preset sensitivity; distinguish relay use from coverage gain and event scores from landscape extent; split rendered RF cells at actual terrain triangles; require successful texture load and image decode for each acquisition.
